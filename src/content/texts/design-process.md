Every design process is, at its core, a way to reduce uncertainty. We start with an ill-defined problem and, step by step, turn ambiguity into decisions that can be built, tested, and refined.

## Start with the problem, not the solution

It is tempting to jump straight into the interface. But the best solutions come from a clear understanding of the problem — of who lives it and why it matters to the business.

- Talk to the people who actually use the product.
- Write the problem in a single sentence before drawing any screen.
- Only then explore directions.

## Prototyping is thinking

A prototype is not the end of the process, it is a tool for thinking together. It makes ideas tangible enough for the team to disagree about something concrete — and that is exactly where learning happens.

### Iterate in short cycles

Short cycles keep the cost of being wrong low. Fail early, fail cheap, learn fast.

When the process works, the outcome does not feel like magic: it feels inevitable.
